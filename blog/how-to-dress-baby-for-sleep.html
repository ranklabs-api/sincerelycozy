How to Dress Your Baby for Sleep: A Season-by-Season Guide for Comfortable Nights

Ask any new parent what keeps them up at 2 a.m., and it isn't always the baby. It's the quiet, second-guessing question that follows every tuck-in: Is she too hot? Too cold? Did I layer her right?

Dressing a baby for sleep is one of those things no one gives you a straight answer on, because the "right" answer changes with the season, the room, and the baby. But once you understand the simple logic behind it, it stops being a guessing game. Here's what we've learned from our own experience as moms — and from talking with hundreds of Sincerely Cozy families.

Start with the Room, Not the Outfit

Before you reach for another layer, check the room. Most pediatric guidance points to a comfortable sleeping temperature of around 68–72°F. The thermostat matters more than the outfit, because a baby's body isn't great at regulating its own temperature yet.

The single most important rule: overheating is the risk to avoid. A baby who is slightly cool will usually fuss and let you know. A baby who is too warm may sleep too deeply — and that's the scenario safe-sleep guidance warns against.

The One-Layer-More Rule (With a Catch)

You've probably heard the classic advice: dress your baby in one more layer than you would wear to be comfortable in that same room. It's a helpful starting point — but the swaddle changes the math.

A swaddle is a layer. So if you're wearing a short-sleeve shirt to bed, your baby likely needs just a light onesie plus the swaddle, not a onesie, a sleeper, and a swaddle. When in doubt, underdress by half a layer and adjust. You can always add, but overdressing is harder to spot.

A Quick Seasonal Cheat Sheet

Warm weather (spring and summer): A short-sleeve cotton onesie or even just a diaper under the swaddle is often plenty. This is where a breathable swaddle earns its keep — a lightweight bamboo fabric lets air move, so your baby stays covered without getting swampy.

Cool weather (fall and winter): Add a long-sleeve footed sleeper under the swaddle, and keep the room in that 68–72°F range rather than cranking the heat. If your home runs cool, one extra layer inside the swaddle beats piling blankets on top of it — loose blankets don't belong in the sleep space anyway.

Between seasons: This is the trickiest stretch. A onesie plus a swaddle usually works, and the back-of-the-neck check (below) will tell you fast if you're off.

How to Actually Check (Hands and Feet Lie)

Here's the tip that saves the most worry: cold hands and feet are normal. A baby's circulation is still developing, so their hands and feet often run cooler than the rest of them. Don't use them as a thermometer.

Instead, feel the back of the neck or the chest. That's a much more accurate read of core temperature:

  • Feels comfortable and slightly warm? You nailed it.
  • Damp, sweaty, or flushed cheeks? Too warm — remove a layer.
  • Cool to the touch on the chest? Add a light layer.

Why Fabric Choice Does Half the Work

You can get the layers exactly right and still have a miserable night if the fabric traps heat. That's why we chose 95% bamboo rayon for every Sincerely Cozy swaddle.

Bamboo is naturally thermoregulating. It wicks moisture away from the skin and lets air circulate, so your baby stays at a steadier temperature whether it's July or January. It's also breathable enough that a swaddle works as a replacement for heavier blankets rather than an addition to them — which keeps the sleep space simple, safe, and comfortable.

The same breathability is what makes bamboo a great pick for sensitive skin. Fewer trapped layers of heat and sweat means less irritation, fewer rashes, and a calmer baby.

The Swaddle That Adapts

One of the quiet perks of a breathable swaddle is that it works across seasons without you having to rethink your whole system. Keep the swaddle the same; adjust what's underneath. That consistency is also good for your baby — the familiar snug wrap becomes its own sleep cue, no matter the weather.

For cooler months, we tend to reach for a footed sleeper and keep the swaddle snug. For warmer months, a light onesie or diaper underneath is usually all it takes.

The 2 a.m. Confidence Check

Dressing your baby for sleep will never be a perfect science, and you'll probably still peek at the monitor more times than you'd like to admit. But now you have a framework:

1. Set the room to a comfortable 68–72°F.

2. Dress baby in roughly one more layer than you — counting the swaddle.

3. Feel the back of the neck, not the hands.

4. Choose a breathable fabric so you're not fighting the temperature all night.

That last point matters more than most people realize. A thermoregulating, breathable swaddle doesn't just keep your baby comfortable — it gives you one less thing to second-guess at 2 a.m. And for a new parent running on very little sleep, that's worth everything.
