How to Swaddle a Baby: A Step-by-Step Guide for New Parents

If you've ever watched a newborn jolt awake the moment you finally set them down, you already know why swaddling matters. That little startle reflex — the one that sends their arms flying and their eyes wide open — is the reason so many new parents find themselves re-wrapping the same blanket at 3 a.m.

A good swaddle feels like a gentle hug. It mimics the snug, secure feeling of the womb and keeps those flailing arms from waking your baby up. But getting it right takes a little practice. The good news? Once you learn the technique, it becomes second nature — and it's one of the simplest things you can do to help your little one settle.

Here's exactly how to do it, step by step.

Before You Start: Pick the Right Swaddle

The blanket you choose matters as much as the technique. You want something large enough to wrap securely — a 47" × 47" swaddle gives you plenty of fabric to work with and stays put better than a smaller receiving blanket. You also want fabric that's breathable and a little stretchy.

Bamboo gives just enough stretch to pull the wrap snug without making it stiff, and it's naturally breathable so your baby won't overheat. The stretch is what makes the difference between a wrap that feels secure and one that feels like a wrestling match.

The Basic Swaddle: Five Simple Steps

Start with your baby calm and the swaddle laid out flat. Here's the method we use (and teach to every new parent we talk to):

1. Fold the top corner down. Lay the blanket flat and fold one top corner down about 6 inches, creating a straight edge. Place your baby face-up with their shoulders lined up just below that folded edge — never with the fold covering their neck or face.

2. Pull the first side across. Take the corner on your baby's left and pull it snugly across their chest, tucking it under their right side. Keep their arm down by their side as you do this. The key word is snug, not tight — the wrap should hold their arm in place without squeezing.

3. Bring the bottom up. Fold the bottom corner up over your baby's feet, leaving their legs and hips room to bend. Their legs should be able to fall into a natural frog position, never pressed straight and bound together.

4. Pull the final side across. Take the remaining corner and wrap it across your baby's chest, tucking it underneath to secure everything in place. This last fold is what locks the whole wrap together.

5. Do the two-finger check. Slide two fingers between your baby's chest and the swaddle. If they fit comfortably, the wrap is just right. If you can't get them in, it's too tight — loosen and re-wrap.

Common Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

Wrapping too tight around the hips. Your baby's hips and legs need room to bend up and out. Tight wrapping around the lower body can put pressure on developing hips, so always leave the bottom loose enough for that natural frog position. Secure the arms and chest, leave the legs free.

The "Houdini" escape artist. Some babies work an arm out no matter how carefully you wrap. If that's yours, try pulling the fabric a touch firmer across the chest, or switch to a swaddle that lets your baby sleep arms-up — many babies settle better that way. The goal is comfort, not restraint.

Relying on a heavy blanket instead of a good wrap. More layers isn't the answer. A breathable bamboo swaddle keeps your baby at a comfortable temperature without the risk of overheating, so you don't need to pile on the fabric.

When to Stop Swaddling

Swaddling is for the newborn stage. The moment your baby starts showing signs of rolling — usually around 2 to 4 months — it's time to transition out of a full swaddle. Rolling while swaddled is a safety risk, so this is a milestone to watch closely. When it happens, move to one arm out, then both arms out, then a sleep sack.
