## What Is Safe Swaddling, Really?

Safe swaddling isn't complicated, but it does take intention. At its core, it means wrapping your baby snugly enough that the startle reflex won't wake them, but not so tightly that their hips and legs can't move freely. The hips should always have room to bend up and out — like a little frog position. Tight swaddling around the legs and hips can contribute to hip dysplasia, and that's something every parent wants to avoid.

The swaddle should sit firmly across the chest and arms but leave breathing completely unrestricted. You should be able to slide two fingers between the swaddle and your baby's chest. If you can't, it's too tight.

## Why Material Choice Is a Safety Decision

Not all swaddle fabrics are created equal, and this is where bamboo really shines. Sincerely Cozy swaddles are made from 95% bamboo rayon, and that's not just about softness — it's about safety too.

Bamboo is naturally breathable. Unlike synthetic fabrics that trap heat, bamboo allows airflow, which helps regulate your baby's body temperature. Overheating is a known risk factor for SIDS, and breathable fabrics are one of the simplest ways to reduce that risk. Bamboo is also hypoallergenic — it won't irritate sensitive newborn skin or trigger eczema flare-ups the way some synthetic blends can.

Temperature regulation matters year-round, but especially in those tricky seasonal transitions when the nursery runs warmer than you expect. A breathable swaddle gives you one less thing to worry about.

## When to Stop Swaddling — and How to Know

This is the question we hear most often from parents. The short answer: stop swaddling as soon as your baby shows any signs of rolling over, or by about 3-4 months, whichever comes first. Rolling over while swaddled is dangerous because the baby can't use their arms to push up or reposition.

The signs aren't always dramatic. Sometimes it's a tiny shoulder rock during tummy time, or a half-roll you catch on the baby monitor. If you're seeing any rolling attempts at all, it's time to transition.

## Common Questions About Swaddle Safety

**Can my baby sleep in a swaddle all night?**
Yes — as long as you're following safe sleep guidelines: baby on their back, firm mattress, no loose blankets or stuffed animals in the crib, and the swaddle secured properly. Check on your baby periodically, but you don't need to wake them.

**What if my baby hates being swaddled?**
Some babies resist at first, especially if they're used to having their arms free. Try swaddling with arms up (some babies prefer this position) or start during naps when they're already drowsy. If your baby consistently fights the swaddle and seems uncomfortable, they might be ready to transition earlier than expected.

**Is it safe to swaddle in summer?**
Absolutely — with the right material. Bamboo's breathability makes it an excellent choice for warm weather. Avoid heavy fleece or thick cotton in summer months. A lightweight bamboo swaddle like ours keeps your baby covered without overheating.

## Getting Started with Safer Swaddling

If you're new to swaddling or want to double-check your technique, here's a simple checklist:

1. **Back to sleep** — always place your baby on their back
2. **Hips free** — leave room for legs to bend and move
3. **Two-finger test** — check snugness at the chest
4. **Watch the temperature** — choose breathable fabrics and dress baby lightly underneath
5. **Know when to stop** — first signs of rolling mean it's time to transition

Safety doesn't mean sacrificing comfort. In fact, the safest swaddles are often the softest — because a comfortable baby sleeps better, and a well-rested parent makes safer decisions too.
